  proof: Choose finite singular d-cycles c_M and c_S on the compact manifolds M_0 and S_0 representing their fundamental classes [M_0] and [S_0]. Since deg(f_0)=D, we may choose a finite singular (d+1)-chain b on S_0 such that
  f_{0*}(c_M)-D c_S = partial b.
  For each simplex appearing in c_M, c_S, and b, choose one lift to X or S and then sum all Gamma_0-translates of those chosen lifts. Because the original chains are finite and the Gamma_0-actions on X and S are properly discontinuous, these sums are locally finite chains; denote them by c_X, c_{S,lf}, and b_lf. The chains c_X and c_{S,lf} represent the locally finite fundamental classes [X]_{lf} and [S]_{lf}.
  Since q_S o f = f_0 o q_M and f is Gamma_0-equivariant, applying f to each lifted simplex of c_M gives the corresponding lifted simplex of f_{0*}(c_M). Therefore f_*(c_X) is exactly the locally finite lift of the finite chain f_{0*}(c_M). Lifting the identity f_{0*}(c_M)-D c_S = partial b term-by-term gives
  f_*(c_X)-D c_{S,lf} = partial b_lf.
  Hence f_*[X]_{lf}=D[S]_{lf} in locally finite homology. This is precisely the defining degree relation for a proper map between oriented non-compact d-manifolds, so the proper degree of f is D.
